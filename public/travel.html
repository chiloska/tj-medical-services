Getting here

Most patients fly into San Diego and are at our clinic within an hour. Here is exactly how the trip works.

Our two locations

San Diego office
480 Frontera Way, Suite 210, San Ysidro, CA 92173, USA. About 5 minutes from the San Ysidro port of entry. Consultations, pre-op appointments, post-op checks and the shuttle to Tijuana. Free parking for patients.
Tijuana clinic
Paseo de los Héroes 9000, Torre TJMS, Floor 4, Zona Río, 22010 Tijuana, B.C., Mexico. About 15 minutes by car from the San Ysidro crossing. All procedures, surgery and imaging take place here.

Arriving by plane

Fly into San Diego International Airport (SAN). Surgery patients are met at arrivals by our driver, who takes you to the San Diego office or straight to the clinic. The drive from SAN to the border is about 25 minutes without traffic.

You can also fly into Tijuana International Airport (TIJ), which has domestic flights within Mexico, or use the Cross Border Xpress (CBX) bridge from its San Diego terminal directly into the Tijuana airport. We pick you up at TIJ either way.

Arriving by car or on foot

You can park at our San Diego office and take the free shuttle, which leaves every hour on the hour from 7:00 a.m. to 3:00 p.m., Monday to Saturday. Reserve a seat when you book. Dental and check-up patients often walk across at San Ysidro; tell us and a driver will meet you on the Mexican side.

We do not recommend driving your own car into Mexico after surgery. If you do drive in, you need Mexican auto insurance; U.S. policies generally do not cover you in Mexico.

Crossing back into the United States

Waits at the San Ysidro and Otay Mesa ports of entry can be several hours at busy times. Eligible patients receive a free medical quick-crossing pass, issued by the City of Tijuana, for the medical lane. Passes are only given to patients treated at our clinic and are never sold. Lane hours are set by the city and can change, so your coordinator confirms them and plans your return time.

Documents to bring

  • U.S. citizens: valid passport, passport card, or SENTRI/Global Entry card
  • Canadian citizens: valid passport
  • Other nationalities: passport plus any U.S. visa your nationality requires
  • Lawful permanent residents: green card and passport

Requirements are set by U.S. Customs and Border Protection and Mexican immigration authorities and can change. Check them before you travel.

Where you stay

Surgery patients recover at partner hotels in Zona Río, a short walk or drive from the clinic, with daily nurse visits. Dental patients can add a hotel package or stay on the San Diego side and use the shuttle each day.

Money and phones

U.S. dollars are widely accepted in Tijuana, and all clinic prices are in dollars. Most U.S. and Canadian mobile plans work in Mexico; check roaming with your carrier. Tijuana uses Pacific Time, the same as San Diego.
