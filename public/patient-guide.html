Patient guide

What to do before you travel, during your stay and after you go home.

Before you travel

  • Complete the medical questionnaire your coordinator sends, and share recent X-rays, lab results or photos.
  • Surgery patients: get any tests your surgeon requests (usually blood work and an ECG if you are over 40) and send the results at least 7 days before travel.
  • Tell us about every medication and supplement you take. Your doctor will tell you which ones to stop and when — do not stop prescribed medication on your own.
  • Stop smoking and vaping at least 4 weeks before any surgery; it raises the risk of complications.
  • Weight-loss surgery patients follow a 2-week pre-op liquid diet provided by our nutritionist.
  • Buy travel insurance that covers medical travel, if you want coverage for cancelled flights or extended stays.

What to pack

  • Passport or other border-crossing document
  • Your medications in their original containers, and a written list
  • Loose, front-opening clothes and slip-on shoes
  • Phone charger and any CPAP machine you use
  • Credit card or funds for the balance of your treatment

During your stay

Your coordinator meets you on arrival and is at every appointment. Before any procedure you sign an informed-consent form in English or Spanish, after your doctor explains the risks and answers your questions. Surgery patients stay 1 to 2 nights in hospital, then move to the recovery hotel with daily nurse visits.

After you go home

  • You leave with your records, imaging, operative report and medication instructions in English.
  • Follow-up calls at 1 day, 1 week, 1 month and 3 months, plus visits at our San Diego office if needed.
  • Wait at least 7 days after body surgery and 3 days after weight-loss surgery before flying long distances, unless your surgeon says otherwise.
  • Call the 24/7 on-call number on your discharge card with any question about your recovery.
  • Go to an emergency room or call 911 right away for chest pain, trouble breathing, heavy bleeding, fever above 101°F (38.3°C), or severe pain or swelling in one leg.
